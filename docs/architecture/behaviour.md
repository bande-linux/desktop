# Behaviour decisions

Where a spec is silent, the principles in [`../design/DESIGN.md`](../design/DESIGN.md) decide.
macOS is the quality reference: where the principles do not settle a question, Bande looks
at how macOS handles it and asks whether a simpler answer is better. Each decision taken that
way is recorded here with its macOS precedent, so that it is not reopened by accident.

- **Background clipboard access**: macOS treats reading the pasteboard without a user paste as a per-app privacy permission, while copy and paste by the user always work. Bande keeps `wl_data_device` and primary selection as they are, since both already require keyboard focus, and offers `ext-data-control-v1`, the background read path, on one connection only: the compositor creates a socket pair, starts `/usr/bin/bande-clipboard` with the other end as `WAYLAND_SOCKET`, and marks its own end as the clipboard daemon. A connection from the listening socket is never trusted, whatever executable or pid is behind it, so a process of the same user cannot obtain data control by exec'ing the daemon binary or starting it with `LD_PRELOAD`. The daemon sets `PR_SET_DUMPABLE` to 0, so other non-root processes cannot ptrace it or read its memory. If the daemon exits, the compositor restarts it, at most once every 10 seconds, launchd's default throttle.
- **Clipboard persistence**: the macOS pasteboard keeps its contents after the copying app quits. `bande-clipboard` reads every MIME type of each new clipboard and primary selection and offers it as its own, so a paste works after the source has exited.
- **Primary selection**: macOS has no primary selection; Linux users expect it, and it never gets in the way of the clipboard, so Bande offers it everywhere without a setting. Text selected in a toolkit text field or text view (by pointer, once the button is released, or by keyboard) or in the Terminal becomes the primary selection (`zwp_primary_selection_v1`), shared with GTK and Qt apps; password fields never offer theirs. A middle click in an editable field pastes it where the click lands, moving the caret there and focusing the field, and in the Terminal pastes it at the prompt (bracketed when the program asks), unless the program has taken the mouse. A middle click anywhere else (buttons, read-only text, the bar) does nothing.
- **Keyboard focus**: closing or hiding the focused window focuses the frontmost remaining window of the same app; if the app has none, no window is focused, as a macOS app stays active without a key window. When the app quits, the frontmost window of another app is focused, as macOS activates the next app. Focusing a window raises it. An app that is not focused does not take focus by opening another window; the window opens behind the focused one. An app's first window takes focus, standing in for app launch until `xdg_activation_v1` is implemented. A click focuses and raises the window under the pointer; a click on the empty desktop leaves no window focused, as clicking the desktop makes the Finder the active app.
- **Menus never outlive a focus change**: an open menu holds the pointer and the keyboard for its app, as on macOS, but only until something else is chosen. A click beside it closes it and is consumed (the next click acts); and every change of the key window or surface that the compositor makes, whatever its cause, closes the menu first (`xdg_popup.popup_done`) and hands keyboard and pointer to the new target: `Super+Tab` and ``Super+` ``, every Super shortcut except the input source switch and the full-screen screenshot (macOS keeps a menu open for its screenshot keys, so a menu can be captured), the overview, the launcher or another shell surface taking the keyboard, an app brought forward from the taskbar, a notification or an activation token, a newly mapped window that takes focus, a workspace switch, a workspace or overview swipe, the desktop pinch and the lock screen. Menus of the taskbar and the desktop follow the same rules. A menu its app destroys, or whose app dies or stops responding, never keeps the pointer or the keyboard: the grab ends with the last menu and the keyboard returns to the menu's window. A drag that starts from inside a menu closes the menu, and a second app asking for a grabbing menu while one is open gets `popup_done` at once.
- **Popup placement**: every menu and popup stays whole on screen, as on macOS, where `NSMenu` opens up from the pointer near the bottom, to its left near the right edge, flips submenus to the side with room, and gives a menu taller than the screen scroll arrows instead of letting it run off; Windows does the same for `TrackPopupMenuEx` (the alignment flags only state a preference the system overrides to keep the menu visible), and GTK (`GdkPopupLayout` anchor hints) and KDE solve it through the very `xdg_positioner` rules Bande uses. Each placement states where it wants to be and how it may yield, and the compositor solves it: a menu at the pointer opens below and to the right of it and flips up or left when it does not fit; a menu below a rect (menu bar titles, pop-up buttons) opens below it and flips above; a submenu opens to the right of its item, overlapping its menu by 2 pt with its first row level with the item, and flips to the left only (it never flips up, it slides, so the item stays beside it); whatever still does not fit then slides along the edge, and a menu still taller than the room is shortened, never below three rows, and scrolls. A shortened menu shows a 20 pt band with a chevron at each end that has more items; resting on a band scrolls at 300 pt/s until the end, the wheel and trackpad scroll it, and keyboard navigation (arrows, Home, End, type-select) scrolls the chosen item into view, so every item stays reachable. A submenu that opened on the left keeps the safe triangle toward it, so a diagonal move to it does not switch rows. The room is the display that holds the anchor (not the whole layout and not the display of the parent window), minus the bars' exclusive zones: a popup never covers the bar unless it belongs to the bar (the bar, the menu bar and the apps strip) or to a full-screen window. This holds for popups of windows, of nested popups and of layer surfaces (the desktop, the bar, Control Center, the Notification Center); the compositor learns a layer popup's parent only after `xdg_surface.get_popup`, so it places it again when the layer adopts it. Control Center and the Notification Center fill the area beside the bar and hear their bar item's rectangle in that area's coordinates, so they open next to the item and clamp to the display.
- **Escape cancels drags, moves and resizes**: DESIGN §2. While a drag and drop is in flight, Escape cancels it as a refused drop does: the target gets `leave` and never a drop, the source gets `cancelled`, and the icon slides back to where the drag began; the button still held then does nothing until it is released. While a window is being moved (title bar, server-side frame or `xdg_toplevel.move`) or resized, Escape puts it back where and as it was: the starting position and size, a tiled window back into its tile and a zoomed one zoomed, any snap preview withdrawn without snapping, and the grab ends. In both cases the Escape press and release are not delivered to the app. macOS cancels a drag with Escape; it has no way to cancel a window move, which Bande adds since a mistaken drag into a snap zone is otherwise only undone by hand.
- **Drag and drop between apps**: as on macOS, the drop's target proposes the action and modifiers override it: Alt copies and Super moves (Option and Command on macOS), read live, so pressing or releasing one while the pointer rests changes the drop at once. Ctrl, which the Wayland spec suggests for copy, does not change a drop. The pointer carries the outcome: the `copy` shape for a copy, the plain arrow (`move`) for a move and `no-drop` where nothing accepts the drag, and the drag icon is drawn on every display it crosses. Selected text in a text field or text view is a drag source (a press on the selection that moves 4 px starts it; a click there without moving places the caret) and editable fields are drop targets that show an insertion caret under the pointer and insert at it, selecting the dropped text. Text dragged within one field moves; dragged into another field or app it copies, as on macOS, unless Super is held, which moves it out of the source. A drop that is never confirmed (`dnd_finished`) does not keep the source waiting: 2 seconds after the drop the toolkit treats the drag as ended without an action, so nothing is deleted. A drop whose data is empty (its source died) is treated as no drop. An app that dies while its drag is in flight, or while it is the drop target, leaves no grab behind: the next click reaches the window under the pointer. A target can accept only copies; then Super does not turn its drop into a move, so dropping a file on the Terminal never removes it from the Files window it came from.
- **Window and app shortcuts**: the compositor keeps `Super+W` and `Alt+F4` (close the focused window), `Super+Q` (ask every window of the focused app to close), `Super+H` (hide the focused app), `Alt+Super+H` (hide every other app), `Super+Tab` (the app switcher, below), ``Super+` `` (the next window of the focused app, below) and `Super+F3` and `Super+F2` (move the keyboard to the taskbar or the menu bar), `Alt+Super+Space` (next input source), `Super+Alt+Escape` (open Force Quit, `bande-monitor --force-quit`, or bring it forward if it is already open), as `Command+W`, `Command+Q`, `Command+H`, `Command+Option+H`, `Command+Tab` and `Command+Option+Escape` do on macOS; other `Super` chords reach the focused app. Inside Bande's own apps `Ctrl+W` closes the window and `Ctrl+Q` quits, handled by the app (Terminal uses `Shift+Ctrl+W` and `Shift+Ctrl+Q`); window menus show Close as `Alt+F4` and Quit as `Super+Q`. `Super+F11` (and Super with the Mission Control key) shows the desktop, as Command-F11 and Command-Mission Control do: the windows slide aside as the four-finger spread pushes them, the keyboard leaves the key window, and the same keys, a pinch or activating an app bring them back. `Alt+Super+N` opens or closes the Notification Center and `Alt+Super+C` Control Center (macOS's Fn-N and Fn-C, which a PC keyboard lacks): the compositor sends `bande_shell_v1.toggle_panel` (since 18, which replaces the old `toggle_control_center` event; the menu bar's request still names Control Center) and the shell surface that draws the panel opens it. Every bound Super shortcut, `Shift+Super+5` (screenshot and recording options), `Alt+Shift+Super+arrows` (move the window to the display beside it), ``Shift+Super+` `` and the Mission Control key included, is a row of the one table in `bande-keyboard`, which Settings ▸ Keyboard lists and a compositor test runs against the key handling.
- **App switcher**: as Command-Tab, `Super+Tab` picks the most recently used other app and switches to it when Super is released, so a quick press switches at once and never flashes the switcher. Held for 150 ms, a card appears centred on the display with the pointer: the running apps' icons in recency order (the active app first, windows without an app id grouped by connection), a well behind the selection and the app's name below; `Tab`, `Shift+Tab` and Left/Right move (the selection glides on the `quick` spring), hovering selects and a click switches. `Q` quits the selected app, `H` hides it (releasing Super then leaves it hidden), `Escape` and a click beside the card cancel, `Return` switches without waiting for the release. `Down` or `` ` `` shows the selected app's windows on every workspace as live thumbnails of their own buffers (minimised and hidden ones dimmed), `Up` goes back to the apps; releasing Super then brings the chosen window forward, unhiding and unminimising it. Switching to an app activates it as the taskbar does: all its windows come forward and, when all are minimised, the latest returns, which Command-Tab does not do. The card fades and scales in from 96 % on the `quick` spring (a fade only under Reduce Motion), uses the `overlay` surface with the overlay shadow and no scrim, since it is transient and does not block, and is published to assistive technologies as a list in a window named App Switcher whose focused item is the selection.
- **Same-app windows**: as Command-backtick, ``Super+` `` (the key above Tab, whatever the layout) brings the focused app's next window forward at once, ``Shift+Super+` `` the previous one, among its windows on the active workspace of every display; one held Super walks the order it started with, so repeated presses reach every window. Held for 150 ms, a strip of the app's window thumbnails shows the walk; a click on one picks it.
- **Window memory**: as macOS restores a document window where it was, an app's first window opens with the size and position its last window had when it closed, remembered per app id and per set of connected displays in `$XDG_STATE_HOME/bande/windows` (one line per app: displays, app id, frame), and moved and shrunk into the visible area of the display it lands on. A tiled, zoomed or full-screen window is remembered by the frame it returns to, so it reopens floating. Dialogs and other windows with a parent, apps without an id and further windows of a running app are not affected; those cascade 28 pt down and right from the app's frontmost window. The size is offered in the first configure, or in a second one when the app names itself only after it, so it costs the app no extra redraw.
- **Fill the other half**: as Snap Assist in Windows (macOS 15 guesses with Fill & Arrange instead, which the user cannot steer), tiling a window to the left or right half with `Ctrl+Super+Left/Right`, a drag to the edge or the window menu offers the free half, unless a window already fills it: a panel there (`overlay` surface, radius 14, scaling in from 96 % on the `quick` spring, a fade under Reduce Motion) shows the display's other windows on the workspace as live thumbnails arranged as in the overview. Hovering or the arrow keys and `Tab` move a ring, a click, `Return` or `Space` tiles that window into the free half and focuses it, and `Escape`, a click elsewhere (which still reaches what it hits), any Super shortcut or the tiled window leaving its half dismiss the panel. Keys typed while it shows do not reach the app.
- **Tiling menu on the zoom control**: as macOS 15 shows Move & Resize when the pointer rests on the green button, and Windows 11 its Snap layouts on the maximise button, resting the pointer on a toolkit window's zoom control for 500 ms, or pressing and holding it as long, opens the window menu's Move & Resize items below the control: Left, Right, the four quarters, Fill (zoom), Move to Next Display (`bande_app_v1.place_window` with `next_display`, since 6, offered only while more than one display is connected) and Return to Previous Size. Each runs the compositor's own tiling, so the window moves on the same springs as a drag to the edge or `Ctrl+Super+arrows`. A click on the control still zooms at once; a hold that opens the menu does not zoom, and moving off the control before the delay cancels it. Server-side frames (`decoration.rs`) do not offer the menu yet: the compositor has no menu of its own to show it in.
- **Keep on Top**: macOS has no such toggle (only floating panels an app makes itself); KDE and GNOME put it in the window menu, and so does Bande, as a checked Keep on Top item between Move & Resize and Move, sent as `bande_app_v1.set_keep_on_top`. A window kept on top stays in front of every other window on its workspace, for the pointer as well as on screen, even while another window has the keyboard; it stays below the menu bar, the taskbar and other shell surfaces, and the toggle ends with the window.
- **Input sources**: as on macOS, the keyboard layout is one choice for the whole session, not per window, and survives switching apps. The sources are the xkb layouts listed in `keyboard input-sources` (at most four, the number of groups one xkb keymap holds), or the layouts of `/etc/default/keyboard` while that is unset, so a fresh install types as the system was set up; the login window always uses the system layouts. macOS switches with Control+Space, but Ctrl belongs to apps here and Super+Space is the launcher, so `Alt+Super+Space` switches (Alt being the alternate modifier, the way Option does on a Mac): a quick press returns to the previously used source, as Control+Space does, and further presses while Alt and Super stay held walk through the list; `Shift` walks backwards. The switch works while the session is locked, so a password is never typed with the wrong layout. The new source's name appears for a second in a small card in the middle of the display you are typing on (the one with the pointer when nothing has the keyboard) and fades out. The compositor tells shell clients the list and the active source (`bande_shell_v1.input_sources`) and takes `select_input_source`; the menu bar shows the active source's short name (such as SV or EN) only when more than one source is configured, its menu lists the sources with the active one checked and ends with Open Keyboard Settings…. Input methods (fcitx5, ibus) are not offered as sources yet.
- **Keyboard and pointer settings**: Settings ▸ Keyboard lists the input sources in order with their short label, moves them up and down, removes any but the last, and adds a layout from a search (the readable names, short labels, countries and languages of xkeyboard-config's `evdev.xml`; every word must match a name, code, country or language, so "swedish mac" finds Swedish (Macintosh); an empty search suggests the layouts of the region's country; Return adds the first match, Escape stops); four is the most. Below are key repeat, what the Caps Lock key does (Caps Lock, Escape, Control, Compose or nothing, as macOS's Modifier Keys offer) and a read-only list of the desktop's keyboard shortcuts by area, taken from the same table (`bande-keyboard`) that a compositor test checks against its shortcut handling. Settings ▸ Mouse appears while a mouse or pointing stick is connected and Settings ▸ Trackpad while a trackpad is (read from udev's device properties in `/run/udev/data`, watched so the panes come and go as devices are plugged in). Mouse has the primary button (left or right), natural scrolling (off by default: most Linux users expect a wheel to scroll the traditional way), tracking speed and pointer acceleration. Trackpad has tap to click (on by default, as the research recommends), dragging (off, tap and drag, or tap and drag with lock, shown while tap to click is on), secondary click with two fingers or in the bottom right corner, natural scrolling (on by default: the trackpad moves content like a touch screen), scrolling with two fingers or along the edge, tracking speed, pointer acceleration and ignoring the trackpad while typing. The compositor applies each change to every libinput device at once and to devices plugged in later, setting only the options a device supports. libinput offers no scroll speed setting, so there is none.
- **Accessibility**: Settings ▸ Accessibility gathers the switches macOS spreads over its Accessibility pane, each written to `accessibility { ... }` in `config.kdl` and served to apps: Vision (Screen reader, Zoom, Increase contrast, Reduce transparency, Reduce motion, Pointer size 24–96 px), Hearing (flash the screen when an alert sound plays) and Motor (Sticky, Slow and Bounce keys); `bande-settings accessibility` opens it. The portal serves `contrast` and `reduced-motion` in `org.freedesktop.appearance`, `reduce-transparency` and `text-size` (`text-size 100`–`200`, not yet followed by the toolkit and not in Settings) in `org.bande.appearance`, and `text-scaling-factor` and `cursor-size` in `org.gnome.desktop.interface`, so toolkit, libadwaita and Qt apps follow contrast and motion live. `Super+F5` starts or stops the screen reader (Orca), as Command-F5 does VoiceOver. Bande ships no screen reader for now: `bande-desktop` Recommends only `at-spi2-core`, and the Screen reader switch, `Super+F5` and its row in the shortcuts list appear only when an `orca` executable is on `PATH` (otherwise `Super+F5` reaches the app). The switch is remembered as `screen-reader`, so the screen reader starts again at login, and turns itself off when Orca quits. The compositor serves `org.freedesktop.a11y.KeyboardMonitor` on `org.freedesktop.a11y.Manager`, the interface Mutter offers, so Orca hears every key (key echo) and its own commands (the Orca key, Insert or Caps Lock, with any other key, and the keystrokes it grabs) never reach the app; a grab ends when Orca leaves the bus. Orca 48 reads `DISPLAY` and runs `xkbcomp` at start, so the compositor starts it with an empty `DISPLAY`; it then still needs `xkbcomp` installed. Zoom magnifies the whole display around the pointer, which stays where it would be unzoomed while the picture pans under it, and follows keyboard focus to a newly focused window until the pointer moves: `Alt+Super+0` turns it on or off (macOS's Option-Command-8 is the workspace shortcut here), `Alt+Super+=` and `Alt+Super+-` step by 1.25× (16× at most), the level glides on the `quick` spring (jumps under Reduce Motion) and everything the compositor draws is rasterised at the zoomed scale, so it stays sharp at fractional levels; the level is remembered as `zoom`. Reduce transparency puts an opaque `chassis` behind every surface that asks for blur. The pointer size reloads the cursor theme and exports `XCURSOR_SIZE` for apps started afterwards. An alert sound (`AlertPlayed` from `bande-sound`, even when muted) flashes the display white for a moment. Sticky keys latch a modifier pressed alone until the next key (twice locks it, a third press releases it); pressing Shift five times asks in a notification whether to turn them on or off. Slow keys accept a key only after it is held for 300 ms; bounce keys ignore a key pressed again within 300 ms of its release. Every toolkit window publishes its AccessKit tree on AT-SPI; `toolkit/platform/tests/screen_reader.rs` starts at-spi2-core, reads a window with a button, field, pop-up button, table and menu through AT-SPI and operates them.
- **Notification presentation**: as on macOS, a notification appears as a banner and is kept in the Notification Center after the banner leaves, so `bande-notify` advertises the specification's `persistence` capability. A banner stays for 5 seconds, or until the notification expires when the sender asks for a timeout; an expiry of 0 or the server default keeps the notification in the center until the user or the sender closes it. A critical notification stays on screen until it is dismissed, as a macOS alert does, and a `transient` one is never kept: it closes as expired when its banner leaves. Notifications are grouped by their `desktop-entry` hint, or their app name without one, into stacks ordered newest first, both on screen and in the center, as macOS groups by app. A replacement keeps its id and is presented again, as a macOS notification re-posted with the same identifier is. Do Not Disturb (the `DoNotDisturb` property of `org.bande.NotificationCenter` on the daemon's object) works like a macOS Focus: notifications go to the center without a banner, critical ones still break through, and banners missed meanwhile are not replayed when it ends. The model (`bande_notify::Center`) is driven by an injected clock and publishes each change as a `Presentation` of banner stacks and center stacks (`PresentationChanged` on `org.bande.NotificationCenter`, also `GetPresentation`), which `bande-notifications` draws. While the pointer rests on a banner (`HoldBanners`) no banner leaves and none expires; when it moves away every banner stays at least 2 more seconds. Swiping a banner away (`HideBanner`) keeps the notification in the center, except a transient one, which closes. A click (`Activate`) emits `ActivationToken` and then `ActionInvoked` with `default` when the sender offers it; otherwise it removes the notification (a resident one only leaves the screen). `Dismiss`, `Clear` (one app) and `ClearAll` close with reason 2.
- **Night Light**: as Night Shift, `night-light { on schedule from to warmth }` in `config.kdl` (read only on `org.bande.Config` as `NightLight` for now; Settings ▸ Displays ▸ Night Light comes with the Settings restructure) warms every display from sunset to sunrise (the sun at the time zone's main city in `zone.tab`, through `bande-datetime`'s `daylight`, which also drives the automatic appearance) or between two custom times (`schedule "custom"`, `from "22:00"`, `to "07:00"`, crossing midnight). `warmth` 0–100 runs from 5000 K to 2700 K (50 by default). The compositor sets each CRTC's gamma ramp (the legacy gamma call, which atomic drivers turn into `GAMMA_LUT`) from a white point normalised to 6500 K, so the ramp is the identity when Night Light is off. At the scheduled times it fades over a minute, switching it on or off fades in 1.5 s, and a new warmth applies at once so a slider previews it; while fading it wakes every 50 ms and otherwise only at the next change (at most every 15 minutes, and again after waking from sleep). A display whose driver offers no gamma ramp (gamma size 0, such as virtio-gpu in a virtual machine) is left alone with one log line; a ramp is set again after a session switch or wake, once the first frame after the reset has flipped. There is no colour-matrix (CTM) fallback yet.
- **Sleep when idle**: a desktop Mac turns its display off after 10 minutes of inactivity and, with "Prevent automatic sleeping when the display is off" unset by default, sleeps at the same moment (`pmset` `sleep 1`, `displaysleep 10`), asking for the password on wake. Bande's lock stage is the display turning off, so without `idle suspend-after` the computer suspends when the session locks, and follows `lock-after` when that changes; an explicit `suspend-after` delays it and `"never"` turns it off.
- **Sleep and wake**: a Mac asleep shows its lock screen the moment it wakes, never the desktop, and is ready at once. Every way to sleep (Sleep in the system menu and in Control Center's power menu, idle, closing the lid, and a press of the power button, which `bande-power` maps to sleep with a logind drop-in, `HandlePowerKey=suspend`, as a Mac's power button sleeps) asks logind to suspend, so all of them run one sequence:
  1. *Before sleep.* `bande-power` always holds a logind `delay` inhibitor for sleep. On `PrepareForSleep(true)`, unless the session is already locked, it asks logind to lock it; the compositor starts `bande-lock`, sends `ext-session-lock` `locked` once a blank frame is on every display, and sets logind's `LockedHint` only once a frame showing the lock screen has been presented on every display. `bande-power` waits for that property change (a signal, no polling), at most 3 seconds, and then releases the inhibitor; a lock screen that never draws leaves the displays blank rather than holding sleep back. The compositor also suspends its input devices, so keys and buttons held into sleep are released.
  2. *On wake* (`PrepareForSleep(false)`, also followed by the greeter's compositor). `bande-power` takes a new inhibitor. The compositor resets every display's DRM state, so the next frame is a full commit of the mode, planes and cursor; rescans the connectors, so a display plugged in or out during sleep is added or removed and the arrangement follows, and a connector whose EDID now names another display (another desk's monitor on the same port) is set up afresh for the new one; restarts every display's frame scheduler, so a page flip lost in sleep cannot stall it; redraws every display; reopens the input devices; and counts the wake as activity, so idle countdowns start from the wake and the display does not dim right after it (an idle dim is undone). A frame a display refuses is dropped and logged instead of ending the session.
  3. *Clocks.* Monotonic time stops during sleep, so frame, animation and idle timing use it (sleep is not idle time), and nothing that follows the wall clock does: the bar's and the lock screen's clocks wake on a `CLOCK_REALTIME` timer with `TFD_TIMER_CANCEL_ON_SET`, which the kernel fires on resume, so neither shows the time sleep began; notification expiry runs on `CLOCK_BOOTTIME`, so a banner whose time ran out during sleep is gone on wake (it stays in the Notification Center) and a transient notification that expired is closed with reason 1. Wi-Fi, Bluetooth and the battery follow NetworkManager, BlueZ and UPower signals, which those services send as they come back.
- **Shell clients**: the compositor starts `bande-shell` and `bande-screenshot` itself, as launchd starts the Dock and Spotlight for each session, on socket pairs, and restarts them at most once every 10 seconds; a crashed shell comes back without the session or its apps noticing. Only those connections see `bande_shell_v1` (`crates/platform/protocols`: `show_launcher` on `Super+Space`, `activate_app`) and `ext-foreign-toplevel-list-v1`, and their activation tokens are valid without keyboard focus, as the taskbar may activate any app. `bande-shell` is one process for the whole session, as macOS's Dock process also draws Mission Control and Launchpad: with one Wayland connection (sibling surfaces through `Window::open_beside`, on a named display through `Window::open_beside_on`, all driven by `run_surfaces`), one GPU device, one connection per D-Bus bus, one font database and one accessibility adapter per surface, it hosts on every display the compositor names (`bande_shell_v1.displays`, since 15, the main one first) the desktop and the taskbar (or, with a separate menu bar, the menu bar, plus the taskbar as an apps strip on the main display), and once the menu search card, the notification banners and Notification Center, Control Center and the launcher. Its first surface is the launcher's, so a `Super+Space` pressed while the shell is still starting opens the launcher; the other surfaces follow with the display list already known, each opened once. It opens and closes a display's surfaces as displays come and go, reopens any surface the compositor closes, and rebuilds a display's taskbar in place when its arrangement changes. The clock and the Control Center button ask the compositor (`toggle_panel`), which relays `toggle_panel` to every shell client, so opening one panel closes the other and puts banners away into the Notification Center. Session-ending confirmations, About windows and the desktop's delete confirmation run as short-lived `bande-shell` child processes. The lock screen is a process of its own, started when the session locks, so the shell can never hold the session open; `bande-compositor --greeter` starts only `bande-greeter`.
- **Notification banners and center**: the shell (`bande-notifications`) shows each app's newest banner in the clock's corner, as Control Center opens there: beside a side bar stacked up from the clock (newest nearest it) and sliding out from under the bar's edge, above the clock at the right on a bottom bar, and at the top right below a top bar or the menu bar sliding in from the screen edge, all with the `standard` spring; an app with more shows the count and a stacked edge. Buttons show the sender's actions except `default`, which a click on the banner runs; without one the click brings the sender's app forward (or opens it). Hovering shows a × that clears the notification; a drag or flick back the way it came, towards the clock (`momentum`), puts the banner away into the center, and a banner that times out leaves the same way. The Notification Center slides in at the right from the clock: stacks newest first, a stack expands on click with Show Less and a clear button for the app, Clear All at the top, each card's age, and a line saying Do Not Disturb is on. Arrow keys and Tab walk it, Enter opens or expands, Delete clears, Escape or a click beside it closes it. Banners are not shown while it is open.
- **Menu bar** (the separate layout, `bar { menu-bar "separate" }`, and every edge but the left while the combined bar is left-only): as on macOS, the Bande mark at the far left opens the system menu (About This Computer, System Settings…, Force Quit… which runs `bande-monitor --force-quit`, Sleep, Restart…, Shut Down…, Lock Screen, Log Out *user*…), then the active app's name in bold and its menus. Restart, Shut Down and Log Out ask first in a small window that acts by itself after 60 seconds, as macOS's confirmation does; Enter confirms and Escape cancels. The apps that are open are reopened at the next login, as macOS's "Reopen windows when logging back in" does by default: the compositor writes their ids to `$XDG_STATE_HOME/bande/reopen` whenever any client asks to end the session (Cancel forgets them), and, through a logind `shutdown` delay inhibitor, when the computer shuts down or restarts without asking (the power button, `systemctl poweroff`), unless the confirmation already wrote them; the shell reopens them at start. At the right sit the apps' status items, then Bluetooth, sound, Wi-Fi and battery when the computer has them, Control Center and the clock. Every item opens its menu on press: Wi-Fi lists known networks before others with the one in use checked (a secured network never joined opens the Wi-Fi pane of Settings to ask for the password), sound offers Mute and the output devices, Bluetooth the paired devices to connect or disconnect, battery the level, the time left or until full and the power source. Scrolling over the sound item changes the volume. While a menu is open, moving to another item opens that item's menu. The clock shows the weekday, date and time as the `LC_TIME` locale writes them (CLDR data through ICU4X in `bande-datetime`, `en-US` for the C locale), and opens the Notification Center, where a notification older than today says Yesterday, its weekday or its date in the same locale; the Control Center item toggles Control Center. Battery percentage is not shown in the bar, as macOS's default; it is the first line of the battery menu. The menu bar and the apps strip are opaque on the frame tone (Datum); so are notification banners (`overlay` cards), the Notification Center (a full-height `chassis` sheet beside the bar holding the cards) and Control Center (a `chassis` panel with the person row, Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, Do Not Disturb and Microphone tiles, fader sliders and Now Playing on `sheet` modules). Only the launcher and the overview sit over a blurred `scrim` (`ext-background-effect-v1`).
- **Taskbar** (the default; `bar { edge size names auto-hide menu-bar apps }` in `config.kdl`, followed live through `BarEdge`, `BarIconSize`, `BarNames`, `BarAutoHide`, `BarMenuBar` and `BarApps` on `org.bande.Config`). Settings ▸ Taskbar offers exactly Position, Size (icon 32–56 pt), Show names, Automatically hide and Menu bar (in the taskbar or separate, at the top). The combined bar is drawn on the left edge for now; any other edge keeps the separate menu bar at the top with the apps on that edge. The shell draws one opaque taskbar on the left edge of every display, `icon size + 24` pt wide (64 by default) on the frame tone with a hairline on its inner side, whose cells reach the screen edge and corners. From the top: the Bande mark (click: the launcher through `bande_shell_v1.show_launcher`, since 12; right click: the system menu), then the apps exactly as in the apps strip below (kept, a hairline, running; badges, drag to reorder or remove, drops, icon menus, launch pulse, minimise targets) with an accent tick at the edge for the active app and a 3 pt square for the others that run. When the apps do not fit, the icons shrink once by 4 pt and the last cell becomes "+N", which lists the rest. The bottom group never scrolls: the Trash, the workspaces as dots with the active one a pill (only with two or more; a click opens the overview), the status notifier items two per row with the input source badge (beyond three a `•••` lists the rest), one Control Center button holding Wi-Fi, sound, Bluetooth and battery glyphs from the icon theme's symbolic set recoloured to the text colour (a click opens Control Center, a right click on a glyph opens that item's menu, scrolling changes the volume), and the clock as the time over the short day ("14:32 / Thu 29", a 12-hour locale writes "PM · Thu 29"), with an accent dot while the Notification Center holds notifications, or a muted moon while Do Not Disturb is on (click: Notification Center; right click: a menu with Do Not Disturb, Notifications Settings… and Date & Time Settings…). Under the mark sits the active app's header: its 24 pt icon over its name in bold and a `›`, dimmed on displays that do not hold the key window (`bande_shell_v1.active_output`, since 12). A click on it, or Super+F2, opens one cascading menu beside the taskbar: a "Search menus" field (typing anywhere in the menu filters every enabled item of every submenu into one list that reads "Edit › Find" with its shortcut; Return runs the first or the chosen result, Backspace and Escape walk the search back), the app row (the app's exported first menu, or for apps that export nothing About *app* greyed, Hide *app*, Hide Others, Show All and Quit *app*), then each exported menu (File, Edit, View, …) as a row opening its submenu to the side, or for apps without menus their desktop-entry actions and their windows. A pointer moving diagonally from a row toward its open submenu keeps that submenu open (a safe triangle that settles after 300 ms), as macOS menus do; the keys walk it as every menu. Menus open beside the taskbar. Super+F2 and Super+F3 reach the taskbar's layer (namespace `bar`) when no `menubar` or `dock` layer exists. Shift+Super+/ (the macOS Help-search chord) opens the menu search: a centred card like the launcher (an overlay layer surface, namespace `menu-search`, that takes the keyboard) listing every enabled item of the active app's menus as "Edit › Find" with its shortcut, ranked by how early the typed text starts a word of the item; Up, Down, Page Up and Page Down move, Return or a click runs the item through the same path as the cascade, Escape clears the text and then closes, and a click beside the card or losing the keyboard closes it. With Show names (`names #true`) the taskbar is pinned open at 248 pt: a search field beside the mark (opens the launcher), the header with the app's icon and bold name and its menus listed as rows that each open their menu, the apps as rows with their names and badges and the active app tinted, the workspaces as numbered pills, the status well with the network and battery level written out, and the time at 22 pt over the long date. Dragging the taskbar's inner edge (the outer 4 pt, with a column-resize pointer) past the middle between both widths pins it open or collapses it and saves `BarNames`. With auto-hide the taskbar reserves no space, slides out to the edge leaving a 2 pt strip (with a 2×24 pt accent sliver at the bottom corner while notifications are unread), slides in after the pointer rests 120 ms on the strip and leaves 450 ms after it, never while one of its menus is open. When the display shows a full-screen window (`bande_shell_v1.full_screen`, since 14) the taskbar moves to the overlay layer and hides the same way, but waits 300 ms so games and video do not reveal it by accident. A click on a workspace dot switches to it (`switch_workspace`, since 14); a click on the active one opens the overview. The taskbar reports where its Control Center button and its clock are (`set_panel_anchor`, since 14), and the compositor relays them in display coordinates (`panel_anchor`), so Control Center opens beside the button, bottom-aligned, and the Notification Center as a sheet beside the taskbar, each sliding out from the taskbar's edge; on a top or bottom bar they open towards the middle, right-aligned with their item. The compositor also re-announces `active_output` when the key window moves to another display, so the header dims on the right displays.
- **Control Center**: a card beside its bar item (or below the menu bar at the right when no bar reported one) whose first row is the person (an initial disc and their full name) with Lock Screen (logind), a power button whose menu offers Sleep, Restart…, Shut Down… and Log Out *user*… (the same confirmations as the system menu) and System Settings, then switches for Wi-Fi, Bluetooth and Do Not Disturb, the network in use (a click lists networks to join), display brightness where a backlight exists and keyboard brightness where the keyboard is lit (both set through logind), the volume and, with more than one output, a pop-up button choosing the output. With a microphone, a Microphone switch (off mutes the input), the input's live level (a capture stream that runs only while the panel is open, shown in decibels and falling back smoothly) and, with more than one input, a pop-up button choosing it. When an app plays media through MPRIS, a Now Playing card follows: artwork (the app's icon when the player gives no local artwork), title and artist, previous, play/pause and next (dimmed when the player cannot), and a position bar with elapsed and remaining time that seeks on click or drag when the player knows the track's length; a click on the card brings the player forward. The card shows the player that most recently started playing; with several players a pop-up button picks another, which then also takes the media keys. The play/pause, pause, next and previous keys reach Control Center through `bande_shell_v1.media_key`, also while the session is locked, and control the same player without showing anything, as on a Mac. It fades and slides in; Escape or a click beside it closes it. The volume keys show a small level card at the same place for 1.5 seconds that never takes the pointer or the keyboard.
- **Apps strip** (the taskbar when the menu bar is separate; the rules also hold for the apps area of the combined taskbar): a floating opaque strip on the frame tone with a hairline edge, on the configured edge showing the apps kept in it (`bar { apps ... }` in `config.kdl`, followed live through `BarApps` on `org.bande.Config`), then behind a thin line the running apps that are not kept, then behind another line the Trash, empty or full (watched with inotify through `bande-fs`). Sizes are in points and icons are loaded at the output scale. A running app has a dot on the edge side; an app being opened gets its dot at once and pulses gently until its first window maps (10 s at most). Hovering an icon shows the app's name beside it after 400 ms; moving to the next icon glides the label there at once. Pressing shrinks the icon; a click opens the app or, when it runs, brings all its windows forward; the Trash opens in Files. Apps appear and leave with their neighbours sliding (`standard` spring), dots and labels fade (`fade`); Reduce Motion keeps only the fades. `com.canonical.Unity.LauncherEntry` updates (as KDE reads them) give a red count badge ("99+" above 99), a progress bar and an urgent state, which pulses the icon twice and keeps an orange ring until the app is activated. The taskbar reports every icon's rectangle with `bande_shell_v1.set_icon_rect`, so windows minimise into their icon. Its input region covers only the strip and the gap to the screen edge, and every icon reaches to the edge, so a push to the edge hits it. Right click, Control-click, holding for 500 ms or the accessibility menu action open the icon's menu beside the taskbar: first the app's windows by title (a minimised one marked ◇; choosing one brings it forward through `activate_window`), then Open (when not running), the actions of its desktop entry (`[Desktop Action]` groups such as New Window, run with their own `Exec` as the jump-list items of a macOS Dock menu), Keep in Taskbar (a checkmark that writes `bar { apps ... }` through the writable `BarApps`), Open at Login (a copy of the desktop entry in `~/.config/autostart`) and Show in Files, then for a running app Hide and Quit (Force Quit while Alt is held, and in place of Quit while the compositor reports the app as not responding); the Trash's menu has Open and Empty Trash…, which asks first in a second menu at the same place naming the number of items and that it cannot be undone, as the Finder's Empty Trash asks. Ctrl+click shows the app in Files. Dragging an icon by more than 4 pt picks it up (the cursor grabs): a kept icon moves along the kept group with its neighbours sliding aside on the `standard` spring, a running app dragged into the kept group is kept where it is dropped, and a kept icon dragged more than one icon width beyond the taskbar shows a Remove label and is no longer kept when released there (a running app stays in the running group; others shrink and fade at the pointer). Kept apps whose desktop entry is missing keep their place in the list. `bar { size 48; auto-hide #true; edge "left" }` are followed live (`BarIconSize` 32–56 pt, `BarAutoHide`, `BarEdge`); a new edge re-anchors the strip, which slides in there. Files dragged over the taskbar (which slides in when auto-hidden) grow the icon they would land on: an app whose desktop entry names any `MimeType` opens them on release, starting if needed, and the Trash moves them to the Trash (Put Back works from Files); other icons refuse the drop. An app asking for attention without
 a valid activation token pulses its icon twice and keeps the ring. Super+F3 gives the taskbar the keyboard (its layer takes the keyboard on demand): the active app's icon (else the first) gets a ring and its label, the arrow keys along the taskbar, Home and End move, Return or Space opens and gives the keyboard back, the Menu key or Shift+F10 opens the icon's menu, and Escape gives the keyboard back to the active app; a click on the taskbar hands the keyboard straight back. With auto-hide the taskbar reserves no space,
 slides out of the screen, and slides back in when the pointer rests 120 ms on the edge strip; it leaves 450 ms after the pointer does, never while its menu is open.
- **Launcher**: as Spotlight, `Super+Space` shows a search card over the screen that takes the keyboard, fading in on the `quick` spring (a plain fade under Reduce Motion) with typing live from the first frame; a second `Super+Space`, `Escape` on an empty query (the first `Escape` clears it), a click beside the card or opening a result hides it at once, and the keyboard returns to the window that had it. Results come from apps (name, then generic name and keywords, then comments; prefix, word, initials, substring and fuzzy matches in that order), System Settings panes (by name, keyword or setting), a calculator with units and percentages (`15% of 80`, `5 km in mi`, `100 f to c`), and folders and documents in the home folder by name. The home folder is indexed once in the background and kept current with inotify, skipping hidden folders and caches (`CACHEDIR.TAG`, `node_modules`); file results stream in without blocking typing. What the user opens ranks higher, weighted by frequency and recency, kept in `$XDG_STATE_HOME/bande/launcher-usage`. The best result is the Top Hit (a calculation always is), then groups follow: Applications, System Settings, Folders, Documents. The selected result is previewed beside the list (text, images, folder contents, size, date and place). `Up`/`Down` select, `Tab` and `Ctrl+Up/Down` jump between groups, `Return` opens (a calculation is copied), `Ctrl+Return` shows a file in Files, `Ctrl+C` copies a calculation or a path, and a right click (or `Menu`, `Shift+F10`) offers the same. Matched letters of a name are drawn bold, and long lists scroll to follow the selection. An empty query shows Recent Apps (from the usage ranking), Recent Documents (`recently-used.xbel` and the launcher's own) and All Apps alphabetically as a grid the arrow keys move through in two dimensions, so there is no separate Launchpad; typing searches at any time. `Shift+Super+V` opens the launcher on the clipboard history, newest first, searchable, with a preview of text, pictures and files; `Return` puts the copy back on the clipboard (and types text into the field when an input method is running), `Delete` removes one and the menu clears all. `Ctrl+Super+Space`, or Emoji & Symbols… in any text field's menu, opens the emoji and symbols grid (CLDR names and keywords in English, compiled in; Frequently Used first; a remembered skin tone from the footer swatches or an emoji's menu); `Return` types the character through text-input when an input method is running, otherwise copies it for `Ctrl+V`. The compositor says which (`bande_shell_v1.open_launcher`, `insert_text`).
- **Terminal**: as Terminal.app's Basic profile: an 80×24 window, a monospace face at 11 px (DejaVu Sans Mono, from which Menlo was derived), its 16 colours with bright colours for bold, 10,000 lines of scrollback, a hollow cursor in an inactive window, Page Up/Down scrolling the scrollback outside the alternate screen, `Alt+Left/Right` sending word movement, `Shift+Ctrl+K` clearing the scrollback, and the shell started in the home folder. Light and dark follow the system appearance live; `Ctrl+=`, `Ctrl+-` and `Ctrl+0` change the text size. A double click selects a word, a triple click a line, Alt+drag a block and Shift+click extends; programs that ask for the mouse get it, and Shift selects instead. `Shift+Ctrl+T` opens a tab and `Shift+Ctrl+N` a window in the current tab's folder (from OSC 7, else the shell's folder); the tab bar appears with the second tab, titled by folder and running program, with `Alt+1`…`Alt+8` (`Alt+9` the last tab), and `Ctrl+Page Up`/`Page Down` to switch. `Shift+Ctrl+F` opens a find bar over the active tab (Enter for older matches, Shift+Enter newer, `Shift+Ctrl+G`/`Shift+Ctrl+H`). `Ctrl`+hover underlines links (OSC 8 or plain URLs) and `Ctrl`+click opens them. Every plain `Ctrl`+letter goes to the shell, so the Terminal's own commands use `Shift+Ctrl` (`Shift+Ctrl+C` copies, `Shift+Ctrl+V` pastes). `Shift+Ctrl+W` closes the active tab (the window with its last tab). Closing a tab with a program still running asks inline in a bar above the text, never in a dialog: Enter closes, Escape keeps it. Touchpad scrolling moves the scrollback by points and glides on after the fingers lift. Files dropped on a pane are typed at its prompt as their paths, single-quoted where the shell would need it and each followed by a space, as Terminal.app does; dropped text is typed as it is, bracketed when the program asks. The Terminal takes drops as copies only and the pointer shows the copy badge over a pane.
- **Event sounds**: macOS plays few, short interface sounds and lets the user turn them down or off; Bande does the same with an original theme following Datum §12 (dry, short wooden mallet and click tones in D major pentatonic, each under a quarter second, mastered to about −20 dBFS RMS), synthesised by `bande-sound` (`crates/services/sound`) and installed as the XDG sound theme `Bande` (`data/sounds/Bande`, regenerated with `bande-sound --write-theme data/sounds/Bande`, which a test checks). `bande-sound` serves `org.bande.Sound.Play(name)` with the freedesktop names (`bell`, `message-new-instant`, `screen-capture`, `trash-empty`, `device-added`, `device-removed`, `battery-low`, `audio-volume-change`) and plays them as short PipeWire streams with the `Event` role, skipped while the output is muted. `config.kdl`'s `sound` section decides: `alert` picks the alert (mallet, knock, low, tick), `alert-volume` scales every event sound, `effects #false` silences the interface sounds (screenshot, Trash, power, battery) and `volume-feedback #false` the tick; alerts and notification sounds stay, as on a Mac. The shell plays: the tick when a volume key changes the level (not while muted and not while dragging a slider), the shutter when a screenshot is taken, the paper rustle when the taskbar empties the Trash, and the plug-in chime when power is connected (unplugging is silent, as on a MacBook). `bande-notify` advertises `sound` and plays a new notification's `sound-name` hint, else `message-new-instant`, unless it has `suppress-sound`, replaces one already shown, or Do Not Disturb holds its banner back; the low battery notification asks for `battery-low`. Settings ▸ Sound puts these four settings first (choosing an alert plays it), shows the input level while the pane is open, and lists the apps playing sound, each with its own volume (all of an app's PipeWire streams together; Bande's own effect and meter streams are not listed).
- **Daemon start**: `bande-notify` is started by D-Bus activation on the first notification, as Debian's notification daemons are. Services that must run for the whole session ship a systemd user unit wanted by `graphical-session.target`, enabled for every user by `deb-systemd-helper --user` in the package's `postinst` as debhelper does, and a D-Bus activation file that defers to that unit through `SystemdService=`; `bande-config`, `bande-power`, `bande-sound`, `bande-tray` and `bande-automount` do so. `bande-clipboard` is started by the compositor, because its trusted connection has to come from the compositor.
- **Opening apps**: every launch path (taskbar, launcher, desktop, Files, Notification Center, Control Center, menus) goes through `bande_xdg::spawn_detached`, which starts the app in its own session and moves it into `app-bande-<id>-<pid>.scope` in `app.slice` through the systemd user manager's `StartTransientUnit`, as systemd's desktop environment integration asks and GNOME does; the id is escaped as systemd escapes unit names (`-` becomes `\x2d`). Without a user manager the app runs unscoped and nothing is shown. An app that ends on a crash signal (SEGV, ABRT, BUS, ILL, FPE, SYS) posts "<App> quit unexpectedly"; one that exits with an error within 5 seconds of opening, or cannot be started, posts "<App> could not be opened". A normal quit, a non-zero exit after it has been open a while, `SIGTERM`, `SIGKILL` (Force Quit) and `SIGINT` post nothing. The notification carries the app's name and icon and is grouped under its desktop entry, so clicking it opens the app again. Apps' stderr stays connected to the launcher's journal stream rather than a pipe, so an app keeps running when the process that opened it quits.
- **App identity**: a window's `app_id` is matched to its desktop entry by `bande_xdg::find_app`, which tries the exact id, the id ignoring case, `StartupWMClass`, the last part of a reverse-DNS id (either side) and the `Exec` program, in that order. The taskbar's apps, app name and About, the switcher and overview, notification senders, the Control Center's player and screenshot names all use it, so `gimp` shows GIMP's icon and name, and the kept `org.gimp.GIMP` lights up instead of gaining a second icon. Calls back to the compositor (activate, hide, quit, window lists) use the window's own id (`running_app`). An id no entry matches shows its last reverse-DNS part. The taskbar then tries the window's process (the compositor sends its pid with `bande_shell_v1.app_process`): its systemd scope when the scope's pid is the window's own, then its executable. Keep in Taskbar resolves an app in that order, entry by app id, trusted scope, executable, and for an app that still matches nothing writes `~/.local/share/applications/<app_id>.desktop` (`Exec` the canonical executable, or the interpreter and the absolute script path; `TryExec` the executable; `X-Bande-Kept=true`; an existing file is never overwritten), which also shows in the launcher and search.
- **Dialogs and their parent window**: macOS keeps a sheet or an app-modal panel with its document window. Bande honours `xdg_toplevel.set_parent` for every window: a dialog opens centred over its parent on the parent's workspace, always stacks directly above it (activating the parent brings its dialogs along), follows the parent's top centre when the parent moves, zooms or tiles, and is minimised, hidden and restored with it; minimising or moving a dialog to another workspace or display acts on its parent. The app switcher card lists apps, so a dialog is not offered there on its own; `` Super+` `` and the overview include it, the overview drawing it on its parent's thumbnail. A dialog marked modal through `xdg_dialog_v1` keeps the keyboard, and a click on its parent's content is not delivered: the dialog comes forward and shakes once on the `shake` spring (a short flash under Reduce Motion), as macOS shakes a sheet. Dragging a server-side title bar of the parent still moves both. A dialog can still be dragged on its own.
- **Tabs in the header**: macOS lets any part of a title bar that is not a control move the window. In a window whose tabs share the header (Terminal), a press on a tab selects it at once, as on macOS, and a drag that starts on a tab and goes beyond the 4 px click slop moves the window; header buttons such as new tab or a toolbar's back button keep the press as a button does. Apps mark these through `Header::tabs` beside `Header::items`. Empty header space and the gaps around tabs move the window as the title bar does.
