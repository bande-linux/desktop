# Behaviour decisions

Where a spec is silent, the principles in [`../design/DESIGN.md`](../design/DESIGN.md) decide.
macOS is the quality reference: where the principles do not settle a question, Unbound looks
at how macOS handles it and asks whether a simpler answer is better. Each decision taken that
way is recorded here with its macOS precedent, so that it is not reopened by accident.

- **Background clipboard access**: macOS treats reading the pasteboard without a user paste as a per-app privacy permission, while copy and paste by the user always work. Unbound keeps `wl_data_device` and primary selection as they are, since both already require keyboard focus, and offers `ext-data-control-v1`, the background read path, on one connection only: the compositor creates a socket pair, starts `/usr/bin/unbound-clipboard` with the other end as `WAYLAND_SOCKET`, and marks its own end as the clipboard daemon. A connection from the listening socket is never trusted, whatever executable or pid is behind it, so a process of the same user cannot obtain data control by exec'ing the daemon binary or starting it with `LD_PRELOAD`. The daemon sets `PR_SET_DUMPABLE` to 0, so other non-root processes cannot ptrace it or read its memory. If the daemon exits, the compositor restarts it, at most once every 10 seconds, launchd's default throttle.
- **Clipboard persistence**: the macOS pasteboard keeps its contents after the copying app quits. `unbound-clipboard` reads every MIME type of each new clipboard and primary selection and offers it as its own, so a paste works after the source has exited.
- **Keyboard focus**: closing or hiding the focused window focuses the frontmost remaining window of the same app; if the app has none, no window is focused, as a macOS app stays active without a key window. When the app quits, the frontmost window of another app is focused, as macOS activates the next app. Focusing a window raises it. An app that is not focused does not take focus by opening another window; the window opens behind the focused one. An app's first window takes focus, standing in for app launch until `xdg_activation_v1` is implemented. A click focuses and raises the window under the pointer; a click on the empty desktop leaves no window focused, as clicking the desktop makes the Finder the active app.
- **Window and app shortcuts**: the compositor keeps `Super+W` (close the focused window), `Super+Q` (ask every window of the focused app to close), `Super+H` (hide the focused app), `Alt+Super+H` (hide every other app), `Super+Tab` (activate the most recently used other app) and `Super+F3` and `Super+F2` (move the keyboard to the Dock or the menu bar), `Super+Alt+Escape` (open Force Quit, `unbound-monitor --force-quit`, or bring it forward if it is already open), as `Command+W`, `Command+Q`, `Command+H`, `Command+Option+H`, `Command+Tab` and `Command+Option+Escape` do on macOS; other `Super` chords reach the focused app.
- **Notification presentation**: as on macOS, a notification appears as a banner and is kept in the Notification Center after the banner leaves, so `unbound-notify` advertises the specification's `persistence` capability. A banner stays for 5 seconds, or until the notification expires when the sender asks for a timeout; an expiry of 0 or the server default keeps the notification in the center until the user or the sender closes it. A critical notification stays on screen until it is dismissed, as a macOS alert does, and a `transient` one is never kept: it closes as expired when its banner leaves. Notifications are grouped by their `desktop-entry` hint, or their app name without one, into stacks ordered newest first, both on screen and in the center, as macOS groups by app. A replacement keeps its id and is presented again, as a macOS notification re-posted with the same identifier is. Do Not Disturb (the `DoNotDisturb` property of `org.unbound.NotificationCenter` on the daemon's object) works like a macOS Focus: notifications go to the center without a banner, critical ones still break through, and banners missed meanwhile are not replayed when it ends. The model (`unbound_notify::Center`) is driven by an injected clock and publishes each change as a `Presentation` of banner stacks and center stacks (`PresentationChanged` on `org.unbound.NotificationCenter`, also `GetPresentation`), which `unbound-notifications` draws. While the pointer rests on a banner (`HoldBanners`) no banner leaves and none expires; when it moves away every banner stays at least 2 more seconds. Swiping a banner away (`HideBanner`) keeps the notification in the center, except a transient one, which closes. A click (`Activate`) emits `ActivationToken` and then `ActionInvoked` with `default` when the sender offers it; otherwise it removes the notification (a resident one only leaves the screen). `Dismiss`, `Clear` (one app) and `ClearAll` close with reason 2.
- **Sleep when idle**: a desktop Mac turns its display off after 10 minutes of inactivity and, with "Prevent automatic sleeping when the display is off" unset by default, sleeps at the same moment (`pmset` `sleep 1`, `displaysleep 10`), asking for the password on wake. Unbound's lock stage is the display turning off, so without `idle suspend-after` the computer suspends when the session locks, and follows `lock-after` when that changes; an explicit `suspend-after` delays it and `"never"` turns it off.
- **Shell clients**: the compositor starts `unbound-dock` and `unbound-launcher` itself, as launchd starts the Dock and Spotlight for each session, on socket pairs, and restarts them at most once every 10 seconds. Only those connections see `unbound_shell_v1` (`crates/platform/protocols`: `show_launcher` on `Super+Space`, `activate_app`) and `ext-foreign-toplevel-list-v1`, and their activation tokens are valid without keyboard focus, as the Dock may activate any app. The menu bar, Control Center and screenshot tool have since joined them on the same terms; the lock screen is started when the session locks, and `unbound-compositor --greeter` starts only `unbound-greeter`.
- **Notification banners and center**: `unbound-notifications` shows each app's newest banner at the top right below the menu bar, sliding in from the screen edge with the `standard` spring; an app with more shows the count and a stacked edge. Buttons show the sender's actions except `default`, which a click on the banner runs; without one the click brings the sender's app forward (or opens it). Hovering shows a × that clears the notification; a drag or flick to the right (`momentum`) puts the banner away into the center. The Notification Center slides in at the right from the clock: stacks newest first, a stack expands on click with Show Less and a clear button for the app, Clear All at the top, each card's age, and a line saying Do Not Disturb is on. Arrow keys and Tab walk it, Enter opens or expands, Delete clears, Escape or a click beside it closes it. Banners are not shown while it is open.
- **Menu bar**: as on macOS, the Unbound mark at the far left opens the system menu (About This Computer, System Settings…, Force Quit… which runs `unbound-monitor --force-quit`, Sleep, Restart…, Shut Down…, Lock Screen, Log Out *user*…), then the active app's name in bold and its menus. Restart, Shut Down and Log Out ask first in a small window that acts by itself after 60 seconds, as macOS's confirmation does; Enter confirms and Escape cancels. At the right sit the apps' status items, then Bluetooth, sound, Wi-Fi and battery when the computer has them, Control Center and the clock. Every item opens its menu on press: Wi-Fi lists known networks before others with the one in use checked (a secured network never joined opens the Wi-Fi pane of Settings to ask for the password), sound offers Mute and the output devices, Bluetooth the paired devices to connect or disconnect, battery the level, the time left or until full and the power source. Scrolling over the sound item changes the volume. While a menu is open, moving to another item opens that item's menu. The clock shows the weekday, date and time as the `LC_TIME` locale writes them (CLDR data through ICU4X in `unbound-datetime`, `en-US` for the C locale), and opens the Notification Center, where a notification older than today says Yesterday, its weekday or its date in the same locale; the Control Center item toggles Control Center. Battery percentage is not shown in the bar, as macOS's default; it is the first line of the battery menu. The bar, notification banners, the Notification Center and Control Center are translucent over a blur of what lies behind them (`ext-background-effect-v1`; rounded cards are covered by bands, as the Dock does) and nearly opaque where the compositor offers no blur.
- **Control Center**: a card below the menu bar at the right with switches for Wi-Fi, Bluetooth and Do Not Disturb, the network in use (a click lists networks to join), display brightness where a backlight exists (set through logind), the volume and, with more than one output, the output in use (a click lists outputs). It fades and slides in; Escape or a click beside it closes it. The volume keys show a small level card at the same place for 1.5 seconds that never takes the pointer or the keyboard.
- **Dock**: a floating translucent strip, blurring what lies behind it through `ext-background-effect-v1` (a few rects following its rounded corners; without blur it is nearly opaque), on the configured edge (left by default) showing the apps kept in it (`dock { apps ... }` in `config.kdl`, followed live through `DockApps` on `org.unbound.Config`), then behind a thin line the running apps that are not kept, then behind another line the Trash, empty or full (watched with inotify through `unbound-fs`). Sizes are in points and icons are loaded at the output scale. A running app has a dot on the edge side; an app being opened gets its dot at once and pulses gently until its first window maps (10 s at most). Hovering an icon shows the app's name beside it after 400 ms; moving to the next icon glides the label there at once. Pressing shrinks the icon; a click opens the app or, when it runs, brings all its windows forward; the Trash opens in Files. Apps appear and leave with their neighbours sliding (`standard` spring), dots and labels fade (`fade`); Reduce Motion keeps only the fades. `com.canonical.Unity.LauncherEntry` updates (as KDE reads them) give a red count badge ("99+" above 99), a progress bar and an urgent state, which pulses the icon twice and keeps an orange ring until the app is activated. The Dock reports every icon's rectangle with `unbound_shell_v1.set_icon_rect`, so windows minimise into their icon. Its input region covers only the strip and the gap to the screen edge, and every icon reaches to the edge, so a push to the edge hits it. Right click, Control-click, holding for 500 ms or the accessibility menu action open the icon's menu beside the Dock: first the app's windows by title (a minimised one marked ◇; choosing one brings it forward through `activate_window`), then Open (when not running), the actions of its desktop entry (`[Desktop Action]` groups such as New Window, run with their own `Exec` as the jump-list items of a macOS Dock menu), Keep in Dock (a checkmark that writes `dock { apps ... }` through the writable `DockApps`), Open at Login (a copy of the desktop entry in `~/.config/autostart`) and Show in Files, then for a running app Hide and Quit (Force Quit while Alt is held, and in place of Quit while the compositor reports the app as not responding); the Trash's menu has Open and Empty Trash…, which asks first in a second menu at the same place naming the number of items and that it cannot be undone, as the Finder's Empty Trash asks. Super+click shows the app in Files. Dragging an icon by more than 4 pt picks it up (the cursor grabs): a kept icon moves along the kept group with its neighbours sliding aside on the `standard` spring, a running app dragged into the kept group is kept where it is dropped, and a kept icon dragged more than one icon width beyond the Dock shows a Remove label and is no longer kept when released there (a running app stays in the running group; others shrink and fade at the pointer). Kept apps whose desktop entry is missing keep their place in the list. `dock { size 48; auto-hide #true; edge "left" }` are followed live (`DockIconSize` 24–96 pt, `DockAutoHide`, `DockEdge`); a new edge re-anchors the strip, which slides in there. Files dragged over the Dock (which slides in when auto-hidden) grow the icon they would land on: an app whose desktop entry names any `MimeType` opens them on release, starting if needed, and the Trash moves them to the Trash (Put Back works from Files); other icons refuse the drop. An app asking for attention without
 a valid activation token pulses its icon twice and keeps the ring. The compositor rounds the blur behind the strip (`set_corner_radius`); older compositors get the banded rects. Super+F3 gives the Dock the keyboard (its layer takes the keyboard on demand): the active app's icon (else the first) gets a ring and its label, the arrow keys along the Dock, Home and End move, Return or Space opens and gives the keyboard back, the Menu key or Shift+F10 opens the icon's menu, and Escape gives the keyboard back to the active app; a click on the Dock hands the keyboard straight back. With auto-hide the Dock reserves no space,
 slides out of the screen, and slides back in when the pointer rests 120 ms on the edge strip; it leaves 450 ms after the pointer does, never while its menu is open.
- **Launcher**: as Spotlight, `Super+Space` shows a search card over the screen that takes the keyboard, fading in on the `quick` spring (a plain fade under Reduce Motion) with typing live from the first frame; a second `Super+Space`, `Escape` on an empty query (the first `Escape` clears it), a click beside the card or opening a result hides it at once, and the keyboard returns to the window that had it. Results come from apps (name, then generic name and keywords, then comments; prefix, word, initials, substring and fuzzy matches in that order), System Settings panes (by name, keyword or setting), a calculator with units and percentages (`15% of 80`, `5 km in mi`, `100 f to c`), and folders and documents in the home folder by name. The home folder is indexed once in the background and kept current with inotify, skipping hidden folders and caches (`CACHEDIR.TAG`, `node_modules`); file results stream in without blocking typing. What the user opens ranks higher, weighted by frequency and recency, kept in `$XDG_STATE_HOME/unbound/launcher-usage`. The best result is the Top Hit (a calculation always is), then groups follow: Applications, System Settings, Folders, Documents. The selected result is previewed beside the list (text, images, folder contents, size, date and place). `Up`/`Down` select, `Tab` and `Super+Up/Down` jump between groups, `Return` opens (a calculation is copied), `Super+Return` shows a file in Files, `Super+C` copies a calculation or a path, and a right click offers the same.
- **Terminal**: as Terminal.app's Basic profile: an 80×24 window, a monospace face at 11 px (DejaVu Sans Mono, from which Menlo was derived), its 16 colours with bright colours for bold, 10,000 lines of scrollback, a hollow cursor in an inactive window, Page Up/Down scrolling the scrollback outside the alternate screen, `Alt+Left/Right` sending word movement, `Super+K` clearing the scrollback, and the shell started in the home folder. Light and dark follow the system appearance live; `Super+=`, `Super+-` and `Super+0` change the text size. A double click selects a word, a triple click a line, Alt+drag a block and Shift+click extends; programs that ask for the mouse get it, and Shift selects instead. `Super+T` opens a tab and `Super+N` a window in the current tab's folder (from OSC 7, else the shell's folder); the tab bar appears with the second tab, titled by folder and running program, with `Super+1`…`Super+9` and `Super+Shift+[`/`]` to switch. `Super+F` opens a find bar over the active tab (Enter for older matches, Shift+Enter newer, `Super+G`/`Super+Shift+G`). `Super`+hover underlines links (OSC 8 or plain URLs) and `Super`+click opens them. `Super+W` closes the active tab (the window with its last tab). Closing a tab with a program still running asks inline in a bar above the text, never in a dialog: Enter closes, Escape keeps it. Touchpad scrolling moves the scrollback by points and glides on after the fingers lift.
- **Daemon start**: `unbound-notify` is started by D-Bus activation on the first notification, as Debian's notification daemons are. Services that must run for the whole session ship a systemd user unit wanted by `graphical-session.target`, enabled for every user by `deb-systemd-helper --user` in the package's `postinst` as debhelper does, and a D-Bus activation file that defers to that unit through `SystemdService=`; `unbound-config`, `unbound-power`, `unbound-tray` and `unbound-automount` do so. `unbound-clipboard` is started by the compositor, because its trusted connection has to come from the compositor.
